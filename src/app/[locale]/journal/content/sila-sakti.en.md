### Bal and Shakti

There are two things. The first is the state of force, *bal*, and the second is the state of energy, *shakti*. So one is force and the other is power. There is a difference between force and power.

One is that which is motionless, which remains in one place. That is bal, force. And the other is that which is dynamic, which is in motion, which takes part in creation and destruction. That is shakti. That which forms the connection between creation and dissolution, that through which creation happens and through which destruction happens, is bal, force. And both are necessary. One is *shava* and the other is *shakti*. Shava means bal, force.

For example, all the parts of our body are in their places. The heart is in its place, the liver is in its place, the kidneys are in their place, the eyes are in their place. Everything is arranged in its place. And that which stands behind this arrangement of all things is called bal, force.

So the distance you see between the Earth and the Sun – that is bal. What you see around the Sun is bal. What you see around the Earth is bal. What is around every moon, every star and every object, outside its boundaries – that is bal. This means that when the boundary of an object is formed, it arises precisely because of this force.

### That Which Has No Boundaries

And force itself has no boundaries. That is wonderful. Force has no boundaries. It is always an object that has boundaries. And the boundary of this object exists because of force. When this boundary dissolves, the object merges with force. Its boundary disappears. In this way every object has its boundary.

That which has boundaries can be seen with these two eyes. But how will you see something that has no boundaries? That which is visible, which can be seen with two eyes, exists within some boundaries. That is exactly why it is visible. It has its outline, which you can draw on paper. What you can draw on paper, you can see with two eyes. But that which you cannot draw on paper, which has no such outline, is not an object of sight. It is an object of knowledge.

And that is why the havans and other rituals and ceremonies you perform are a method of moving from what can be seen to what can be known. From shakti we arrive at bal. That is why the worship of shakti is necessary, so that we can reach bal, so that we can understand this force. Because bal is knowledge itself.

Bal is spread everywhere. This force is omnipresent. It pervades everything, it is all around us. It has no boundaries that could be measured. The space around the Sun, the space around the Earth, the space in front of every single thing, the space around the whole universe. Such space is everywhere around us. All this space, the whole sky, has no image. And that which has no image, which is omnipresent, cannot fit entirely into some object. And if it did fit entirely into some object, it would no longer be omnipresent. These are two things.

### Ishvara Is Not Born

It is often said that God is born, that Ishvara is born. But Ishvara is never born. God is never born. *Ishvar ansh jiva avinashi.* The living being is an indestructible part of God. In the form of individual parts, things are scattered throughout this whole world.

### Who Steers the Body

I don't know whether you are aware of it or not, but several causal bodies can dwell in a single body. Causal means *chitta*, the conscious mind. And thinking is connected with this chitta. This state exists in every body. There are many struggles and conflicts in it. If you have not worshipped shakti, then there is no one to protect you from these things. Then a person does not even know whose body it is and who is actually controlling it.

It is precisely in the presence of a capable Guru, a perfect Guru, that you can know whether this body belongs to you and you yourself steer it, or whether this body is yours but someone else steers it. When we want to do something in a certain way but cannot, when things do not happen as we would like, and this repeats again and again, it means that someone else is giving the orders.

So each person's state manifests according to their shakti. If the shakti is sufficiently strong, capable and with the person, then the one to whom the body belongs also steers that body. The steering wheel is in their own hands. That is something truly wonderful.

Every day I see many such things that are not described in the shastras, are not in books or in the sacred scriptures. Great spiritual masters have written something, but one's own experience says something else. Some person is very good, but suddenly some evil causal body enters them and their nature changes. Nature means that their subtle body changes. Because what steers the physical body is the subtle body.

The causal body never steers the gross body directly. It has its intermediary, its manager, and that is the subtle body. Through it, it steers the physical body. So someone enters the body of another person, begins to steer it and takes control over it.

### The Power That Grows in the Chakras

But if you have sufficient power of Shakti, if your Shakti is in order, if you are worshippers of Shakti and Shakti is with you, then no other living being can enter your body and move about in it. Then only you yourself are there. Your meditative sadhana belongs only to you, and all the merits you gain through this meditative sadhana belong only to you. These merits stay with you, and your Kundalini Shakti gradually grows more and more. The power keeps increasing and accumulating.

In every chakra a new power arises. Through friction between every two chakras, new energy arises in the subtle realm. When friction of chakras happens in the gross realm, gross power and gross bodies arise. And when your own chakra comes into contact with the next chakra, when they connect with each other and friction occurs between them, a new power arises in the subtle realm. This power then acts according to your instructions, according to your will.

That is why any yajna, fast or religious ritual you perform strengthens your inner power, your spiritual power. It strengthens your will and opens for you the path of meditative sadhana. That is why yajnas, rituals and similar ceremonies are necessary from time to time. They are also very important for a spiritual practitioner.

### Feeling Good and Being Good

But when we say that we liked something, that we did something good and feel good about it, there is still a path from feeling good to truly becoming good. Doing something good and being good are two different things. Feeling good and being good are two different things. When we do something good, we feel good about it. But for a person to also truly become good, for that goodness to really be realised in them, what is needed? For that, meditative sadhana is needed.

When the Darbar says that the mind is purified through bhajan, it means that when you sing bhajans, the mind is being purified. And when you perform service, when you serve cows, when you serve people, help the poor and those who deserve help, when you serve your Gurus and your parents, then through such service the *buddhi*, the intellect, is purified. The buddhi becomes pure.

So **the mind is purified through bhajan. The buddhi is purified through service. And the chitta is purified through meditative sadhana.** Meditative sadhana is therefore the highest means. Through it the purification of the chitta takes place.

### What Purification of the Chitta Means

What does purification of the chitta actually mean? In your chitta there are unnecessary energies that have settled in it and taken control over it. You don't even know about it. But they have settled there. And it is precisely through meditative sadhana that the purification of the chitta happens.

You have surely seen that animals have parasites called ticks. They latch on, bite in with their teeth and hold on. And then people pull them out with tweezers. When they pull them out, a little blood comes out with them. It is similar here. Another living being cannot enter whole, but causal bodies can enter with their own causal body. And all these causal bodies then gradually leave. This is how the purification of the chitta takes place.

Sometimes you have to experience in your own body the consequences of other people's karma. When other beings, their energies and memories, enter a person, then their illnesses and sufferings, their physical, mental and spiritual pains, pass on to the person whose body and life they have entered. And this person then has to experience and endure them. Through purification of the chitta, however, they are freed from this.

### Divine and Demonic Forces

All these evil forces that are present in all of creation, in the whole universe, these forces never, never… So if these forces, if the wrong energy comes into contact with you and then settles in the form of the Mother, then only suffering comes, only illness. Then there is no longer any way forward.

And what do these evil forces do? Everyone who works for dharma, everyone who works for the welfare of people, these demonic forces attack directly. And I have said it repeatedly, I say it again and I will keep saying it: **the divine forces are much weaker than the demonic forces.**

It is the same as in our material world. Imagine that somewhere ninety decent people are sitting and ten criminals with pistols in their hands walk in. Those ninety people will sit quietly and say to themselves: "Brother, do what you want." If those ninety people stood up and rushed at those ten, those ten could do nothing. But they will not stand up. They simply will not stand up. They will not even speak. And it is exactly the same in the subtle world. It is similar there too. The demonic forces are so powerful that they can suppress the divine forces, divine beings and divine men.

### Serving Cows

All who are connected with us, all the people connected with our organisation – many of them are already devoted to the service of cows, *gau seva*. The greatness and spiritual significance of serving cows are not described in books. But we see their significance very well. A cow can sacrifice her own body and give life to a person who is dying. If you create a bond with a cow, if you create a relationship with Gau Mata, Mother Cow, and Gau Mata creates a bond with you, then even Yamaraja, the lord of death, must release you. I see these things directly with my own eyes.

That is why since ancient times, and even fifty years ago, when we were small and saw it ourselves, cows were kept in every house. Every farmer had a cow at home. There was hardly a single house without a cow. And fifty or sixty years ago there were no gaushalas, shelters for cows, because the cows were with every farmer. Cows were respected, people valued them. Cattle were kept and also used in farm work.

And what did this bring? Every household prospered. In families there were no quarrels and conflicts. There were no illnesses. All kinds of problems that arise in life gradually resolved themselves thanks to serving cows. Such families did not break apart.

For the cow has her own, quite special aura. Her aura is completely different. The cow is not an ordinary animal. When we look at her in the right way, she is actually the foundation of all the deities. She is the one who carries within her all the gods and goddesses. That is why serving Gau Mata and a loving relationship with her form an immensely strong protective shield for the whole family.

We see it every day. When someone gets into a really difficult situation, into great danger, and suddenly escapes from it, I look at it and ask: "What happened? How was this person saved?" And then the Darbar shows me that it was the merits gained by serving cows. So I ask those people: "Do you keep any cows? Do you have a cow at home?" And they answer: "Yes, Gurudev, we have cows. We keep two cows and look after them." And then I see that the merits gained by serving cows manifest and save the person.

Serving cows is immensely powerful. It is in no way less significant than serving one's own parents. Whoever has the opportunity to serve their parents should certainly also serve cows. Through this service, duties are fulfilled and one is freed from debts. A person is freed from debts, from karmic obligations towards their parents and from all kinds of other obligations. If a person cannot do anything else, if they cannot do something for the Guru or for their family, but nevertheless serves cows, then through this service too they can be freed from all kinds of debts and obligations. Four kinds of these debts are spoken of, and through this service a person can attain freedom from them.

The very word "cow" is so beautiful and loving. As soon as you say it, love begins to flow continuously from within you. It is enough just to hear the word "cow". But try saying "buffalo" now. Your mood changes at once. But when you say "cow", your mind calms down. By the word alone. By merely looking at a picture of a cow. When you look at a picture of a buffalo or say the name of any other animal, you simply imagine some animal. But as soon as you say "cow", the image of the Mother immediately comes to your mind. It doesn't even occur to you that it is an animal. Within you a feeling arises, love.

That is why *gau seva*, the service of cows, has immense significance. It is comparable to performing yajnas, sacred rituals. It is no small matter.

### How the First Human Was Born

I don't know whether we have ever talked about this together or not. Once I asked Baba: "How was the human being actually born? How was the very first human born?" And Baba answered me immediately. He didn't think for a moment. He said: "Son, the first human was born from the belly of a cow." At the time I let it be. I thought he had simply said it, and I didn't think about it further. I was a student of science; I studied the natural sciences.

But over time I began to understand further connections. When a human child is in the mother's womb, it is nourished by certain microorganisms. And when the child is born, there is also the mother's menstrual blood, from which, according to this explanation, our body arises and the whole human race continues. Our body is formed from the mother's menstrual blood. And yet people call this blood impure.

There are many saints, many people and scholars who consider themselves very learned. They have read many books, have become learned and think they already know everything. And then they say of the mother that she is impure, that she is defiled. And I say to them: "Brother, then you yourself are the most impure of all! After all, your own body arose from your mother's menstrual blood. So there is no one more impure than you!" A mother is, after all, a Mother.

And when the child is born, that same menstrual blood, according to this explanation, comes out of the mother's breasts in the form of milk. The very same microorganisms that nourished us in the mother's womb, thanks to which we could live in her womb, then come out of her breasts in the form of milk. The child drinks this milk and continues to be nourished through the same microorganisms. And then I began to understand that the same microorganisms that nourish us in the mother's womb also nourish us through mother's milk after birth. And these same microorganisms, according to this explanation, are found only in the milk of native Indian cows, desi cows. They supposedly occur nowhere else. And that is exactly why, when a mother has no milk, the child is given cow's milk. And it suits the child directly, the child's body accepts it well. And that is exactly why these people too…

So that is precisely the point. That was also the reason why all our rishis and munis went into the forests. Even though they had their homes and lived in society, they still went into the forests. And great wars were even fought over cows. Whether it was the conflict with Sahasrabahu, the events connected with Bhagavan Parashurama, or the conflict between Vishvamitra and Maharishi Vasishtha, truly great conflicts and wars took place over cows. Such immense significance did the cow have. Truly enormous significance.

But today we have forgotten this significance and the cow has disappeared from our lives. Very unfortunate are those people who cannot serve cows, who do not have the opportunity to perform *gau seva*. Those are truly very unfortunate people.

### Milk That Arises from Love

I myself have seen what influence Gau Mata, Mother Cow, has. I have two sons, and from the first day of their lives they had the good fortune to be able to drink amrita, nectar in the form of milk from native Indian cows, desi cows. And to this day, wherever they go, they drink no other milk. Not only do they not drink other milk, but even if someone brought them any milk dish, for example rabri, a sweet thickened milk dessert, they will not eat it. They cannot eat or drink it. They say it smells bad to them. But when you give them milk from a cow, they drink it at once. That is how strong a relationship they have with it.

And I also realised how very fortunate we ourselves were. Because during my life I drank milk. I drank cow's milk and I also drank goat's milk. In Ayurveda it is said that if cow's milk is not available, you should switch to goat's milk. Then you can switch to buffalo milk or look for other alternatives. But cow's milk… Cow's milk is called mother's milk.

And cow's milk has been given a very beautiful description: "A fluid that arises from motherly love and tenderness." What we call milk, *dugdh*, arises from the feeling of motherly love. If a cow does not love her calf, her milk will not let down properly. But when she lovingly caresses her calf, her offspring, and showers it with tenderness, the milk lets down by itself. And then everyone quickly comes and milks, quickly, quickly. It is the same with a human mother. When her child plays in front of her and she feels even a little motherly tenderness towards it, her milk begins to flow. So milk is a fluid that arises from the feeling of motherly love.

But when you give a cow an injection, give her some preparation and then milk her by force, that is no longer milk. That is poison. Truly poison. That is why the most important thing of all is *bhava*, the inner feeling and disposition. According to the feeling with which the milk arose and came out, that same feeling settles in you too through drinking it. That same feeling remains in you. That is why cow's milk is amrita, the nectar of immortality.

### Glossary of Spiritual and Sanskrit Terms

This glossary explains the main spiritual, philosophical and religious terms that Gurudev uses during his talk and the group meditation. It is intended above all for readers and viewers who are only beginning to get to know the Indian spiritual tradition, tantra and yogic practice. The explanations respect the meaning of the terms in the context of Gurudev's teaching. Where Gurudev uses a specific spiritual interpretation, we distinguish it from the general meaning of the Sanskrit word.

**1. Basic spiritual and philosophical terms**

| Term | Devanagari | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shakti** | शक्ति | Divine energy, the dynamic and creative power of the universe. In Gurudev's explanation it represents the active principle that makes possible origination, movement, transformation and dissolution. It is associated with the Divine Mother and the feminine aspect of the divine. |
| **Bal** | बल | Force, power or firmness. Gurudev uses this term for the omnipresent, motionless principle that maintains the arrangement and boundaries of individual objects. He distinguishes it from the dynamic Shakti. |
| **Brahman** | ब्रह्मन् | The absolute, highest and all-pervading reality of Indian philosophy. Brahman is not limited by space, time or any concrete form. |
| **Ishvara** | ईश्वर | God, the Supreme Lord or the divine principle. In some philosophical schools it represents the personal aspect of the highest reality. |
| **Jiva** | जीव | The individual living being or individual soul. In the spiritual understanding it is associated with consciousness, karma and the cycle of birth. |
| **Maya** | माया | The cosmic power of manifestation, connected with the origination and perception of the manifold world. In the philosophy of Advaita Vedanta it is also associated with the veiling of the knowledge of the highest reality. |
| **Dharma** | धर्म | Cosmic order, right action, duty and spiritual law. In a broader sense it denotes living in harmony with truth and universal principles. |
| **Karma** | कर्म | Action, deed and its consequences. According to Indian spiritual traditions, every action creates consequences that influence the further experiences of a living being. |
| **Bhava** | भाव | Inner feeling, attitude, disposition or spiritual attunement. Gurudev emphasises that a person's inner state is of fundamental importance in prayer, meditation and service to others. |
| **Amrita** | अमृत | The nectar of immortality. In spiritual language it denotes the divine essence, the nectar of bliss or the life-giving principle. |

**2. Consciousness, mind and spiritual bodies**

| Term | Devanagari | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Manas** | मनस् | The mind, above all its sensory, emotional and thinking side. It processes perceptions and takes part in the arising of thoughts, desires and doubts. |
| **Buddhi** | बुद्धि | Intellect, intelligence and the capacity for discrimination. It enables a person to recognise, decide and distinguish right from wrong. According to Gurudev it is purified through selfless service. |
| **Chitta** | चित्त | The inner conscious mind, in which impressions, memories and deeper mental imprints are stored. Gurudev emphasises its purification through meditative sadhana. |
| **Sthula sharira** | स्थूल शरीर | The gross or physical body. The visible and tangible component of human existence. |
| **Sukshma sharira** | सूक्ष्म शरीर | The subtle body. In the yogic and Vedantic traditions it includes the subtle components of the human being, such as the mind, life energy and sensory functions. |
| **Karana sharira** | कारण शरीर | The causal body. The subtlest of the three traditionally distinguished bodies, associated with the deeper causes of individual existence and karmic dispositions. In his talk Gurudev also describes the influence of other causal bodies on a person. |
| **Samskara** | संस्कार | A deep mental or karmic imprint created by past experiences and actions. According to yogic philosophy, these imprints can influence later behaviour and experience. |

**3. Meditation and spiritual practice**

| Term | Devanagari | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sadhana** | साधना | Systematic spiritual practice aimed at inner development, purification of consciousness and spiritual realisation. It may include meditation, mantras, prayers, breathing exercises and other practices. |
| **Dhyana** | ध्यान | Meditation, a state of focused attention and deep inner absorption. |
| **Kundalini Shakti** | कुण्डलिनी शक्ति | Spiritual energy traditionally described as resting at the base of the spine. In tantric yoga its awakening and ascent are associated with spiritual transformation and higher states of consciousness. |
| **Chakra** | चक्र | Literally "wheel" or "circle". In the tantric and yogic tradition it denotes a centre of a person's subtle energy system. |
| **Granthi** | ग्रन्थि | Knot or binding. In the yogic tradition it denotes energetic or spiritual knots that represent obstacles on the path of the ascent of Kundalini. |
| **Brahma granthi** | ब्रह्म ग्रन्थि | The first of the three main energy knots. It is associated with the lower centres and attachment to physical existence. |
| **Vishnu granthi** | विष्णु ग्रन्थि | The second energy knot, traditionally associated with the heart region and the overcoming of emotional and psychological attachments. |
| **Rudra granthi** | रुद्र ग्रन्थि | The third energy knot, associated with the higher centres of consciousness and the overcoming of subtle forms of ego and duality. In Gurudev's talk the name Mahesh granthi also appears. |
| **Kutastha** | कूटस्थ | The unchanging principle of consciousness. In some yogic traditions the term is also used for the point of spiritual concentration in the area between the eyebrows. |
| **Mantra** | मन्त्र | A sacred syllable, sound, word or formula used in meditation, prayer and ritual. |
| **Om / Aum** | ॐ | The sacred syllable representing the highest reality and the fundamental cosmic sound. It is used in meditation, the recitation of mantras and spiritual rituals. |
| **Sankalpa** | संकल्प | A conscious decision, intention or spiritual resolve. In sadhana it denotes a clearly formulated inner determination. |
